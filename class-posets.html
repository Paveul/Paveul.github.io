Posets & Galois Connections

Genesis

My favourite type of mathematical objects are those which are easy to define, yet they give rise to rich complexity. Groups, which I ended up specialising in during my academic life, definitely full into this category and so do posets—which this class is about.

Besides their elegance, posets are also everywhere. Every time there is any sort of hierarchy, or comparison between your objects of interest, or between their elements, posets will come handy as a language to talk about it. The second part of the class title—Galois connections—is a prime example of this naturality.

Finally, what I think makes the topic suitable for a class for talented high-school aged maths enthusiasts is that there are lots of fun problems to think about.

Content & teaching notes

The class was planned for the 3-day $\times$ 80 min format of MBL, but there is much more material in the notes.

The rough plan was as follows: at first the students are guided into coming up with the basic definitions and examples. Next they choose one or two extension topics to dive deeper in, according to their natural preferences. Finally, we all meet together for the Grand Finale, which is examples and the theory of Galois connections.

Among the extension topics I included:

  • lattices – for the algebraically-inclined
  • dimension of posets – for the combinatorial-inclined
  • ordinals & Axiom of Choice – for the logicians & set-theorists
  • Möbius inversion – for the number-theorists
